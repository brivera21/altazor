Quality sources of knowledge that have remained over the years.

### Open Culture

[openculture.com](https://www.openculture.com)

A blog and directory that curates free educational and cultural media from around the web. They have thousands of free online courses, audiobooks, textbooks, ebooks, language lessons, and movies, all organized into browsable index pages. They also run a daily blog of short posts on art, literature, music, film, history, and science, usually pointing to a newly digitized archive, a rare recording, a lecture series, or a public domain film.

I don't remember when I found it. It might have been as early as 2008. I try to keep up with everything they put out, and even after going years without checking in, I can go back and read post after post of things worth knowing about.

### The Marginalian (formerly Brain Pickings)

[themarginalian.org](https://www.themarginalian.org)

Maria Popova's blog and newsletter, built around long form essays on books and on figures from art, science, literature, and culture across several centuries.

I have been getting the newsletter for over ten years and I have saved most of them. Popova's writing has always felt important and worth keeping.

### Brain Food, from Farnam Street

[fs.blog](https://fs.blog)

The weekly newsletter of Shane Parrish's Farnam Street blog. It covers mental models, decision making, learning, reading, and the art of living, under the tagline "mastering the best of what other people have already figured out."

Brain Food has been handing out free gems of wisdom consistently. I look forward to reading it Sundays.

### Oshan Jarow's Musing Mind

[musingmind.substack.com](https://musingmind.substack.com)

Narrower in range than the others: consciousness science, economics, psychedelics, meditation. The pieces are deep dives into a single topic, covering both its history and what is new in it. I have particularly enjoyed the inclusion of novel, at times radical, ideas from social science. They have added knowledge and given me genuine insights.

These works have inspired and motivated me. Like good food, you can feel the thought and care that goes into the words you read and the curation you see. Altazor, like them, is my attempt to add signal to the noisy void.
