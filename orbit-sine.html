Earth circles the Sun once a year, clockwise when seen from south of the ecliptic. With a time axis added, the circle becomes a helix: from the side, Earth simply bobs up and down, and the trace it leaves stretches back through the years as a sine wave. The view swings freely between the pole (circle) and the side (wave).

Note on accuracy: the clockwise direction of both Earth's orbit and its spin is correct for a south-of-the-ecliptic viewpoint, and the side-on projection of uniform circular motion really is a sine wave. The horizontal axis is time, not distance; Earth stays ≈1 au (149.6 million km) from the Sun on a nearly circular path. Sizes are not to scale.
