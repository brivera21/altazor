Lenient scale: sizes and spacing are exaggerated so every planet is visible.
All eight planets lined up on one side of the Sun, a moment real orbits never quite produce. Each planet opens up close, with its numbers: area, mass, moons, what it is made of, when it was discovered and when a probe first reached it. Make to scale sets the true distances and sizes; almost everything left is space. There, a pulse of light leaves the Sun and crosses the diagram in true proportion, carrying its elapsed time, and under each planet sits how long sunlight takes to reach it. The view pans and zooms freely.
