About the map

The lit half is always a half: the shadow's edge is a great circle whatever the date, and the hour only slides it west at fifteen degrees an hour. What the date changes is the lean. The axis is tilted 23.44 degrees, so the point with the Sun overhead runs that far north in June and that far south in December.

In June the shadow clears the Arctic and buries the Antarctic; in December they trade. Running the year holds the clock at one time of day, so the terminator stops sliding and only its lean moves: the Sun draws a slender figure of eight on the map, its width the quarter hour a sundial runs ahead of or behind a clock, and the daylight curve stacks up between its two solstice limits.

The ground wears the colours those climates show from orbit, and the lights that come up behind the terminator are cities, placed and sized by how many people live in them.
