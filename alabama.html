Alabama

A mark under the cursor lands here

The map is the real state in Web Mercator: rivers and lakes from Natural Earth, county lines and recent populations from the Census Bureau's cartographic files, terrain shaded live from the AWS Terrain Tiles, and the woods layer drawn from the USGS National Land Cover Database, forest classes only. Each chip turns one layer on or off; a mark under the cursor fills the card, a click pins it. The border, neighbor names and counties appear from the years they were drawn.

The slider runs from 1492: the nations who lived here first as colored patches, approximate homelands for orientation, their population figures scholarly estimates, then settlements, capitals and removals year by year, while the flag panel shows whose claim covered the land until the official state flag. City circles grow green, then yellow, orange, red as census counts pass 10 thousand, 100 thousand, one million, and the years above the slider jump to the turning points. These nations still exist today; Native Land Digital maps their territories fully, with community input, and is the place to see them properly.
